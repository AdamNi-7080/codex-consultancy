# Engagement lifecycle

Use this lifecycle for a new product direction, design system, architecture choice, or any engagement where the user has material choices to make. It is a decision-support workflow, not a reason to delay a well-specified change.

## 1. Discover

The manager owns the client conversation. Delegates may research, map options, and draft questions, but they do not ask the user for approval or silently decide product direction.

Treat the user's stated outcomes, values, and explicit constraints as direction. Treat a proposed implementation, technology, explanation, or business idea as a candidate hypothesis unless the user explicitly asks to adopt it without further assessment. Do not dismiss the candidate: turn it into focused research questions and compare it fairly with credible alternatives.

When the host is already in Plan mode, use its question flow for material decisions: offer up to three meaningful options with a recommendation and trade-off, while allowing a free-form alternative. When Plan mode is not active, ask one concise material question at a time in ordinary conversation; a skill cannot switch the host into Plan mode.

Ask only what materially changes the plan. Typical themes are audience, desired outcomes, brand and accessibility constraints, delivery scope, technology constraints, ownership, timeline, and success evidence. Record decisions, assumptions, and unanswered questions.

For each material user-proposed candidate, dispatch proportionate research that tests:

- evidence that it can meet the stated outcome and constraints;
- key feasibility, operability, accessibility, security, cost, or maintenance risks relevant to the task;
- the strongest credible alternative or variation; and
- what would change the recommendation.

Use a Mapper, Evidence researcher, Boundary specialist, or a task-specific challenger as appropriate. Do not research a settled preference with no factual or delivery implication, and do not spawn overlapping roles merely to manufacture disagreement. Results must state whether the candidate is supported, qualified, contradicted, or still unproven, with evidence and confidence.

For a future design system, discovery may use a product/design strategist, design-systems architect, and accessibility or implementation-feasibility reviewer only when each passes the delegation-worth-it check. Their job is to produce options and evidence, not an implementation before direction is approved.

## 2. Propose and obtain sign-off

Present a concise engagement proposal:

```text
Outcome and non-goals:
Confirmed decisions and assumptions:
User candidates tested: <candidate | evidence | fit/risk | alternative | verdict/confidence>
Options considered and recommendation:
Proposed milestones and acceptance evidence:
Team and why each role is needed:
Delivery risks, dependencies, and approvals still required:
Goal statement (only if long-running delivery is desired):
```

The user may approve, revise, or reject the proposal. Approval authorizes the agreed delivery scope only; it does not authorize production changes, publication, spending, destructive actions, or other external effects not already in scope.

For a report, incident update, or other communication, the proposal or communication brief must name its destination, audience, approval route, and whether the output is a draft or ready to publish.

## 3. Sustain approved long-running delivery

When the user explicitly asks for a persistent goal, or clearly asks the engagement to continue until a stated verifiable outcome, create a Goal using:

```text
Outcome: <end state, not a list of activities>
Constraints: <scope, approvals, tools, exclusions>
Verification: <tests, review, artifact, or metric proving completion>
Stop condition: <completion or required user decision>
```

Use a Goal after plan sign-off, not as a substitute for it. Keep working through approved milestones until the outcome and verification are complete. Mark it complete only after all stated criteria are met; report a true block only when progress requires a user decision or unavailable external change. A Goal retains the same permissions and approval boundaries as the engagement.

## 4. Deliver and close

Run the approved staffing, waves, acceptance, and handoff controls in the operating model. Return to the user for a new decision when scope, product direction, or an approval boundary changes; otherwise continue through verification. Update the decision log when a user-approved choice changes the plan.
